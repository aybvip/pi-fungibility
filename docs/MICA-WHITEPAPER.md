# FAQ — The official MiCA whitepaper: “Pi is only available through mining”

> Source: **MiCA Whitepaper — Pi Network** (a mandatory document under the EU’s MiCAR regulation).
> 🔗 https://minepi.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MiCA-Whitepaper-Pi-1.pdf
> Published: **2025-11-27** · Start of admission to trading: **2025-11-28** · Home Member State: **Malta**.
> Entities: **PiBit** · **Pi Foundation** · **Pi Network** · **SocialChain Inc.**

---

## The quote people cite (Section G.4)

> **G.4 Future Public Offers**
> “Not Applicable. PiBit does not currently contemplate a public offering of Pi tokens. **Pi
> tokens are only available through mining.** Individuals or entities that have mined tokens
> may elect to sell tokens on the secondary market, but none of the PiBit, Pi Foundation,
> Pi Network, SocialChain Inc. or any affiliates engage in direct selling efforts.”

**What they usually mean:** either
- (a) “So the π on OKX/Bitget isn’t real Pi”, or
- (b) “So there’s a single origin.”

## The answer: the quote itself demolishes the claim ✅

The text says plainly: “Individuals or entities that have mined tokens **may elect to sell tokens on the
secondary market**.”
→ **Exchanges are the secondary market**; the π there **is mined π that someone sold**. Same asset, same origin.

It’s supported by **Section E.2**:
> “The issuer is seeking admission to **secondary trading only**, through which the issuer will not gain any direct proceeds.”
and **Section E.27**:
> “Neither PiBit nor Pi Foundation is involved with the secondary offers and sales of Pi.”

👉 There is no separate “exchange Pi”. There is **one origin: mining** — and exchanges resell it. This **confirms**
fungibility; it does not deny it.

## 🎁 More importantly: the whitepaper hands you 6 stronger proofs

### 1. An explicit classification: “native, fungible”
> **F.2 Crypto-Asset Functionality:** “Pi is the **native, fungible digital asset** of the Pi Network Layer 1 blockchain.”
👉 The official document itself calls Pi **fungible** and **native**.

### 2. Listed in a “Functionally Fungible Group”
> **F.14:** “**Functionally Fungible Group** Digital Token Identifier: **2Z2WZP5KM**”
👉 A standard (ISO 24165 DTI) term placing Pi inside a **functionally fungible group**. (**F.13** is the asset’s
own DTI: `2K95TZ2QN` — an ID for the **asset**, not for units.)

### 3. Immutable — even by the issuer
> **G.3:** “The Pi token and its’ on chain functionalities are **immutable**. No modifications to transactions or the current blockchain ledger history can be made by anyone, **including the issuer**.”
> **Risk section:** “Once deployed, Pi's contract code **cannot be altered, upgraded, paused, or otherwise modified** by Pi Network or any other party.”
👉 **No tagging, no freezing, no modification** — not by Pi, not by anyone.

### 4. No transfer restrictions
> **G.11 Crypto-Assets Transfer Restrictions:** “**Not applicable**”
👉 No origin- or type-based transfer restrictions.

### 5. Native asset + non-custodial wallet
> **F.6:** “Pi Token is the **native digital asset** of the Pi Network…”
> “Pi Network does not maintain custody… has a **native noncustodial Pi Wallet** app…”
> “There are no rights or obligations associated with the Pi token.”

### 6. Transfers on a Stellar model
> **F.2:** “Transfers of Pi occur on the Pi Network using a consensus design adapted from the **Stellar Consensus Protocol**… operates on a **non-custodial** basis…”
